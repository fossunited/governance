# **Education Working Group Charter \[Draft\]**

## **1\. Preamble**

FOSS United's education work aims to make students act as thoughtful citizens who help shape technology. It does not want them to be only the last step in a chain, implementing solutions that others have designed. This means students who understand how the digital systems around them work, who own and govern them, and how they affect people. It also means students who feel able to act on what they learn.

The [National Education Policy 2020](https://www.mhrd.gov.in/sites/upload_files/mhrd/files/NEP_Final_English_0.pdf) describes the purpose of education in similar terms. It speaks of people with sound ethical values who contribute as engaged citizens to an equitable, inclusive and plural society, as the Constitution envisages. The digital commons gives students a practical way to build this habit. Contributing to a commons means working in public, crediting others, following community norms, and sharing responsibility for what is built. These are civic habits as well as technical ones.

For institutions, FOSS and the digital commons can make learning more accessible, lower costs for institutions, and give students room to tinker and build.

Several groups in India already work in this space, but most of this work happens within individual institutions or regions. Colleges that want to adopt FOSS often have no one to learn from. An approach that works in one institution may not suit another, because subjects, infrastructure and resources differ.

The Education Working Group (EDU-WG) gives colleges and universities one place to ask, learn and share. It brings together people with experience in FOSS, the digital commons and education, and institutions that want to take part.

The WG does not just aim to enforce the adoption of open source software. Its work covers:

* awareness and adoption of open software,  
* open education policies,  
* open research policies, and  
* an open culture within institutions.

The WG helps institutions learn from mentors and from each other. It also advises the FOSS United Learners Program on its long-term direction.

## **2\. Status and Authority**

The EDU-WG is established by the FOSS United Foundation with representation from the academic community. It works on promotion and adoption of FOSS and digital commons principles in colleges and universities in India.

The EDU-WG has two functions.

* **Advice.** The WG advises the Learners Program staff at the FOSS United Foundation on the long-term direction of its education work. Staff remain responsible for running programmes day to day.  
* **Mentorship.** The WG supports member institutions as they adopt open source software and practices. Mentors share their experience. Members bring their institution's needs and progress to the group.

Over time, we imagine the EDU-WG working as a national educational Open Source Programme Office (OSPO). As more institutions join, the WG becomes a shared space where they learn from mentors and from each other.

The EDU-WG does not have its own budget. It sends requests for funds to the Foundation. The Foundation approves and tracks all spending.

The EDU-WG reports to the GB. The GB reviews the WG's work and charter periodically.

This charter is not legally binding. It is written in plain language so that everyone in the community can read it.

## **3\. Scope**

The EDU-WG works with colleges and universities in India. It starts with STEM institutions and departments but will expand to non-STEM institutions. At this point, non-STEM institutions and departments are included wherever possible.

Schools are outside the current scope.

## **4\. Mission, Vision and Values**

**Mission.** To increase the awareness, adoption and development of Free and Open Source Software (FOSS) and Digital Commons in colleges and universities in India.

**Vision.** Colleges and universities where learning infrastructure is open, courses encourage collaboration, and students graduate as people who build technology, not only use it.

**Values**

* **Open collaboration.** Educational resources that anyone can use, modify, reuse and redistribute, with credit to their authors.  
* **Inclusivity.** Recommendations that account for differences in infrastructure, region and resources across institutions.  
* **Student empowerment.** Code and digital tools are treated as public goods, so that students start building early.

## **5\. Roles and Membership**

The EDU-WG has a voting core of mentors and one Foundation staff member. Institution members take part in discussions as non-voting participants.

**Mentors (voting)**

* Number of seats: 5  
  * The Governing Board will approve the five initial members who can then add more members through voting.  
* Mentors are invited people with experience in open source and education, such as educators, open source developers, policy makers and other experts who work in the intersection.  
* No more than one mentor from the same institution, company or organisation may hold a seat at the same time.

**Foundation staff and convenor (voting)**

* One seat, held by a staff member of the FOSS United Foundation, who will be the convenor.  
* The convenor calls meetings, keeps notes with help from another staff member (non-voting), tracks attendance, publishes reports, and is the point of contact between the WG, the Learners Program and the GB.

**Institution members (non-voting)**

* Institution members represent colleges and universities that want to adopt open source and receive mentorship from the group.  
* Each institution holds one seat.  
* An institution may replace its representative when needed. The WG decides on each replacement.

**Guests**

The WG may invite other FOSS United Foundation staff, student leaders, institutional partners or external experts to specific discussions. Guests do not vote.

## **7\. Meetings, Quorum and Voting**

The EDU-WG meets once a month, unless the convenor calls an additional meeting.

* **Quorum.** At least 50 per cent of voting members must be present for the WG to take decisions.  
* **Meetings without quorum.** The WG may still meet, but cannot take decisions at that meeting.  
* **Attendance.** Attendance is recorded in the public meeting notes.  
* **Consensus.** Decisions should come from consensus, defined as the lack of sustained objection.  
* **Voting.** If a vote is needed, each voting member has one vote.  
* **Ties.** If a vote is tied, the decision is escalated to the GB.  
* **Non-voting participants.** Institution members and guests take part in discussions but do not vote.

## **8\. Selection, Terms and Active Participation**

**Appointment**

* The GB appoints the initial members, based on recommendations from Foundation staff.  
* After that, the WG may bring in new mentors and institution members.

**Active participation**

Members must stay active to keep their seat. A member is inactive if they miss three consecutive meetings and make no async contribution in that time. The WG may then ask a mentor to step down, or ask an institution to name a new representative.

## **9\. First-Year Goals**

In its first year, the EDU-WG will:

1. Revisit and affirm the framework for working with educational institutions.  
2. Guide existing institution members to adopt open source software and practices.  
3. Develop a handbook for colleges and universities that want to set up centres for open source software and hardware work.

## **10\. Reporting and Transparency**

* The convenor publishes a report on the FOSS United blog after every meeting.  
* Each report records attendance and the decisions taken.  
* The GB reviews the WG's updates periodically, as set out in the GB charter.

## **11\. Licensing of Outputs**

Everything the EDU-WG produces is public and free for anyone to use, with attribution.

* **Handbooks, frameworks and other written material:** [Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 International (CC BY-SA 4.0)](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/).  
* **Code:** an [OSI-approved licence](https://opensource.org/licenses).  
* **Sources:** WG outputs credit for every source they use.

## **12\. Code of Conduct and Conflicts of Interest**

All participants follow the [FOSS United Code of Conduct](https://fossunited.org/code-of-conduct).

* **Advocacy.** Members can advocate for open source alternatives in education. They should not use their position to favour specific consulting firms, proprietary forks, or narrow institutional interests.  
* **Conflicts of interest.** Members declare any interest that could affect their judgement. Examples include a role in a company that sells or supports a tool under discussion, or a decision that directly affects their own institution. A member with a declared interest does not vote on that decision. Declarations are recorded in the meeting notes.

**Violations**

* If a member violates the Code of Conduct, the GB, in consultation with the Foundation and the Community, can request the member to step down.  
* If a member is in gross and intentional violation of the Code of Conduct, the GB, in consultation with the Foundation, can remove them from the WG.

## **13\. Amendments**

* **Proposals.** Anyone in the community or the WG can propose a change. Proposals are sent by email to \[EDU-WG email address\]. Each proposal states the specific change and the reasons for it.  
* **Publication.** The convenor publishes each proposal on the community's communication channels.  
* **Feedback window.** The voting core sets a feedback window for each proposal and announces it with the proposal.  
* **Vote.** After the feedback window closes, the voting core votes. An amendment passes with a two-thirds majority of voting members.  
* **GB sign-off.** Required for changes to the WG's scope, status and authority, or voting structure. Other amendments are shared with the GB once passed.  
* **Annual review.** The WG reviews this charter once a year.  
* **Changelog.** Every amendment is recorded in the changelog with its date and rationale.

## **14\. Dissolution**

The GB, in consultation with the Foundation and the Community, can dissolve the WG or replace its members if:

* its objectives have been met,  
* it is no longer fit for purpose, or  
* it is inactive, meaning it has not met for four consecutive months.

All WG outputs remain public under their licences after dissolution.
